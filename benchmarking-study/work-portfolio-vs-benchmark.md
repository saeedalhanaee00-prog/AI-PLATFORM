## Answer first

The documented portfolio scores a provisional 57 of 100 on the study's scoring model (calculated in session; arithmetic shown below). The profile is sharply asymmetric: investigation methodology, fraud risk assessment and executive reporting operate at Advanced level (4 of 5) — carried by personal method, not by systems — while everything that requires owned infrastructure (whistleblowing channel, case management system, entity-wide conflict-of-interest and gifts machinery) sits at Developing or below. The portfolio is a strong reactive-investigative function with one proactive programme (the Fraud Risk Assessment), and its single largest structural risk is that its maturity is embodied in one practitioner's codified discipline rather than institutionalised in section procedures, systems and staffing.

## 4. Cross-cutting patterns in the body of work

1. **The method is self-correcting.** Five withdrawn findings on case 246200043 were each converted into a permanent collapse test; 17 quality-log entries in ten days became binding principles. This is the mechanism leading practices formalise as "lessons learned / root cause analysis" — it already operates here, at personal level.
2. **The posture is predominantly reactive.** Nine of the ten documented engagements begin from a complaint, a referral or an assigned case. The Fraud Risk Assessment programme is the single proactive, risk-based engagement. The benchmark distinction the study draws — Reactive against Proactive / Intelligence-Led — locates the portfolio on the reactive side with one strong exception.
3. **Maturity is embodied, not institutionalised.** The strongest capabilities (investigation method, verification gates, reporting discipline) exist as one practitioner's codified practice. No section procedure, manual, or second practitioner appears in the record. Key-person dependency is the portfolio's largest single risk and the first thing a Target Operating Model must resolve.
4. **Infrastructure is the binding constraint.** Every dimension scoring 2 or below fails for the same reason: it needs an owned system or an entity-wide programme (reporting channel, case system, declaration platform, gifts register, culture measurement), not better casework.
5. **Prevention exists only as recommendations.** Preventive controls (pre-scoring declarations, role-combination bars, deviation triggers) appear in the record as recommendations issued from casework — not as controls the section operates.

## 5. What this means

- Read against the study's maturity anchors, the function is an Established integrity function (weighted 57) whose investigative core would score Advanced against any UAE peer — but whose score is capped by absent infrastructure, and a benchmarking presentation that averaged this into one number would hide exactly the asymmetry that matters. Present the profile, not the average.
- The gap analysis in the main study (Section 18) starts pre-loaded: the current-state column for Investigations, Fraud Risk and Reporting is strong; for Whistleblowing, Case Management, COI, Gifts and Culture it is thin, and those are precisely the dimensions where external benchmarks are expected to show system-based practice.
- Misreading risk: the 57 must not be reported as an assessment of the Department or of the section's full reality — it scores the documented record held in this environment. Anything the section does that never reached this record is invisible here.
