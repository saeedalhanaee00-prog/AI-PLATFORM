## 1. Dubai Economic Security Centre (DESC) — specialized investigative agency model
- Legal basis: Dubai Law No. 4 of 2016, amended by Law No. 9 of 2019.
- Powers: "supervision, research, investigation, information gathering, taking preventive measures... financial and administrative auditing, as well as recording crimes and offences"; "monitor, examine, investigate, and collect information by all means available"; may "request the Public Prosecution to seize funds, property, documents, papers."
- Crimes: "corruption; crimes of fraud, bribery, embezzlement, Public Funds abuse, counterfeiting, forgery, money laundering, and financing terrorism"; plus illicit gain, profiteering, cheating in obligations with Dubai government entities.
- Intake: public reporting platform; reporter must "provide documentation or evidence of the violation"; anonymous reporting publicized (Gulf News, August 2024).
- Model: standalone specialized centre with law-based mandate. Status: Implemented.
- Sources: dlp.dubai.gov.ae law page · u.ae ESCD pages · wam.ae 1395294615032.

## 2. DFSA — regulator enforcement model (governance benchmark)
- Powers: Regulatory Law Art. 78 (commencing investigations), Art. 80 (compelling information/documents/interviews; written notice required for Art. 80(1)(b)–(e)).
- Methodology: "risk based approach"; acts "fairly, openly, accountably and proportionally"; outcomes: no further action, Enforceable Undertaking, or Decision Notice; unpaid fines recoverable "as a debt due."
- KEY GOVERNANCE FEATURE: Decision Making Committee (DMC) under RPP 7-7 — 1 or 3 members who "should have had no previous direct involvement in establishing the evidence upon which the decision is based" (structural separation of investigators from decision-makers); appeals to the independent Financial Markets Tribunal (FMT).
- Evidenced enforcement: Al Ramz Capital LLC, Decision Notice 24 January 2025 — USD 25,000 fine for "failure to report suspicious transactions" (wash trade on NASDAQ Dubai); referred to FMT; upheld February 2026. Ark Capital Management fined USD 504,000 (February 2026, market-abuse systems failings).
- Model: dedicated Enforcement division; adjudication separated into DMC + external tribunal. Status: Implemented.
- Sources: dfsa.ae about-enforcement + regulatory-actions · dfsaen.thomsonreuters.com Part 5 powers + DMC + 24 Jan 2025 notice.

## 3. ADGM FSRA / Registration Authority
- FSRA conducts formal investigations ("An FSRA investigation found that ACL had contravened..."); RA has "consolidated and enhanced its investigation powers and information gathering powers" with an Enforcement and Disqualification Manual; publishes notices and settlement agreements; "ordinarily publicizes outcomes... to maintain the integrity of ADGM and deter contraventions."
- Evidenced actions: Aarna Capital Limited — USD 504,000 penalty (19 December 2024) for AML systems/controls failures 2017–2023, reduced from USD 630,000 (settlement discount mechanics); Wise Nuqud Ltd — USD 360,000 for AML EDD failures.
- Model: regulator-embedded enforcement. Status: Implemented.
- Sources: adgm.com announcements (Aarna, Wise Nuqud) · adgm.com monitoring-and-enforcement overview.

## 4. CBUAE
- Dedicated AML/CFT Supervision Department (est. August 2020); coordinates with the Examination Division of Banking Supervision and the Enforcement Division "to take disciplinary measures such as imposing fines"; Financial Crime Supervision Department runs skilled-person reviews; examinations involve "detailed testing and extensive interviewing of key staff."
- Evidenced outcomes: AED 200m sanction on an exchange house for AML failures plus AED 500,000 personal sanction on a branch manager with prohibition (individual accountability evidenced); AED 4.1m on three exchange houses (July 2025). Powers from Federal Decree-Law 20/2018.
- Model: supervision → examination → enforcement chain; stage separation at department level. Status: Implemented.
- Sources: centralbank.ae/en/cbuae-amlcft · The National July 2025 · amluae.com (secondary).

## 5. Corporate models — who investigates
- **ADNOC**: Code of Conduct — "coordinates investigations with relevant internal and external subject matter experts, with the compliance officer responsible for ensuring investigations are thoroughly conducted and adequately resolved via correct channels within a reasonable timeframe." Intake via Takallam (third-party). Function sits: Compliance. Implemented.
- **Mubadala** (strongest corporate evidence): "Mubadala's Ethics & Compliance Office is responsible for investigating and remediating alleged violations of law, regulation, Mubadala Group compliance policies, or the Code." ISO 37301 + 37001 + 37002 certified. Function sits: dedicated Ethics & Compliance Office. Implemented.
- **Emirates Group**: harassment/bullying reports "will be thoroughly investigated"; Confidential Disclosure Policy governs whistleblowing; EK Flight Catering hotline third-party operated. WHO investigates: not publicly specified — No sufficient public evidence identified.
- **Etihad**: Ethics Line reports "shared with a Compliance Officer who will decide on the appropriate action." Function sits: Compliance. Implemented.
- **e&**: retaliation reportable to "Human Resources or the Ethics and Compliance Department"; investigation governance beyond this: No sufficient public evidence identified.
- **FAB**: whistleblowing under Regulatory Compliance; categories incl. "fraud and financial misrepresentation, bribery and corruption"; "all reports are investigated fairly and transparently." External datum: QFCRA fined FAB QAR 200,000,000 for obstruction of an investigation into market manipulation (failure to produce documents) — cautionary benchmark on regulator-facing conduct.
- **Emirates NBD**: "the Head of Fraud Prevention and Investigation assigns cases to a competent member under his supervision, who ensures that all reports are filed and evidence is maintained under secure and confidential conditions"; investigations "fairly, objectively and in a timely manner"; no investigator with "a vested interest in the outcome"; discloses report numbers, misconduct types and measures taken in annual reports.

## 6. Public-sector models
- **Federal (Decree-Law 49/2022 + Executive Regulations)**: an Administrative Violations Committee "formed in each Federal entity to consider employee workplace violations"; Executive Regulations "regulate the mechanisms of investigation and disposal of violations"; suspension with salary withholding pending investigation completion. Model: committee-based adjudication inside each entity; investigation procedure codified centrally. Sources: uaelegislation.gov.ae/1594 · fahr.gov.ae Executive Regulations PDF · uaelegislation.gov.ae/1219 (Administrative Violations and Penalties law).
- **UAE Accountability Authority (Federal Decree-Law 56/2023)**: "reviews and undertakes administrative investigation into complaints and reports regarding misappropriation of funds and assets, conflicts of interest, misuse of authority... and exploitation of public office for personal gain"; "refers administrative investigation documents to competent prosecution if the investigation reveals suspicion of a criminal offense"; public-funds recovery mandated. Model: external oversight body with own administrative-investigation arm + prosecution referral gateway. Sources: uaeaa.gov.ae news N19122023 · uaelegislation.gov.ae/2174.
- **Dubai government**: Law 8/2018 (HR Management): "a Work-related Offences Committee will be formed in each department." Executive Council Resolution 4/2019 (Disciplinary Penalties, Grievances and Complaints Code): director general may form one or more Committees of Administrative Violations that must "adjudicate the violations and issue a decision within 30 days" (one extension), "unless the employee is referred to a competent judicial authority"; Grievance Committee appeal within 5 working days. Sources: dlp.dubai.gov.ae Law 8/2018 + ECR 4/2019 · Khaleej Times.
- **Dubai Financial Audit Authority (Law 4/2018, amended by Law 24/2024)**: mandate "to detect any illegal activities related to public funds and investigate all violations"; auditees must refer specified incidents to the Authority; scope includes "mismanagement or misappropriation of public funds, abuse of office... bribery... forging documents"; 2024 amendments add travel bans and asset seizure for offending employees; own Central Violations Committee (Resolution 4/2025 ToR). Sources: dlp.dubai.gov.ae Law 4/2018 + Resolution 4/2025 · Al Tamimi · Chambers.
- **ADAA (context only)**: publicly evidences an "Integrity & Anti-Corruption unit" and the Wajib platform (vrp.adaa.gov.ae) for Abu Dhabi entities and >25% government-owned companies. Not profiled per exclusion.
- **Abu Dhabi departmental internal-investigation units**: No sufficient public evidence identified of any individual Abu Dhabi or Dubai government department publicly describing its own internal investigation unit, reporting line, or methodology (beyond HR-law committee structures). THIS ABSENCE IS ITSELF A FINDING: internal investigative practice in UAE government entities is not publicly documented at entity level; public evidence concentrates in oversight bodies and regulators.

## 7. Methodology standards in the UAE context
- ACFE/CFE: no UAE government entity found publicly referencing ACFE standards or CFE-staffed units; evidence limited to a training market and private forensic firms. No sufficient public evidence identified of official adoption.
- Chain of custody / digital forensics at civilian regulators (DESC, DFSA, FSRA, FAA, UAEAA): No sufficient public evidence identified.
- Outsourced-forensics market context: Crowe UAE and peers advertise digital forensics/fraud services in-country.
- Practitioner literature (methodology framing, not entity evidence): GIR "UAE: key considerations for carrying out internal investigations" (2025); DLA Piper on UAE whistleblower investigations.
- ISO, not ACFE, is the publicly-claimed standards currency in UAE corporate practice (Mubadala 37301/37001/37002).

## Synthesis — organizational models actually evidenced in the UAE
1. Regulator-embedded enforcement with structural separation (DFSA, FSRA, CBUAE): investigators in a dedicated function; sanction decisions separated (DMC; Examination vs Enforcement; FMT appeals). The most fully documented investigation-governance model in the UAE.
2. Standalone specialized investigative agency (DESC).
3. External oversight body with investigation arm + prosecution referral (UAEAA federal; Dubai FAA; ADAA in Abu Dhabi — context).
4. Committee-based internal adjudication (federal and Dubai HR law): entities adjudicate via Violations Committees; the investigating role preceding the committee is codified in executive regulations but no entity publicly describes a standing investigation unit.
5. Compliance-owned corporate model (Mubadala, ADNOC, Etihad, FAB, e&; Emirates NBD names a Head of Fraud Prevention and Investigation). No UAE corporate found publicly placing investigations in Internal Audit, Legal, HR, or an independent investigations unit. Third-party intake hotlines near-universal.

## Evidence gaps (findings in themselves)
(a) No UAE government entity publicly documents an internal investigation unit's reporting line or methodology. (b) No public UAE reference to ACFE standards, chain of custody, or interview standards by any government entity. (c) Corporate disclosures stop at intake and ownership — methodology, case governance and disciplinary-committee operations are not published; Emirates NBD's disclosure of report counts/outcomes is the transparency high-water mark found.
