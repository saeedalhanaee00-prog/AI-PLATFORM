# Evidence Register — Stream 3: UAE Legal and Regulatory Framework

Date accessed for all items: 2026-09-04. Evidence tier: corroborated secondary (search-service extractions citing official URLs; direct fetch blocked by environment egress policy). No entry is graded "confirmed from official text by direct fetch"; official-source URLs are identified for direct verification.

## Area 1 — Penal Code
1.1 Federal Decree-Law No. 31 of 2021 (Crimes and Penalties): primary criminalisation of bribery across Articles 275–287 — public-sector bribery, foreign public officials, employees of international organisations, private-sector bribery, intermediary liability. Bribe = "undue gift, benefit or grant" in return for performing, omitting, or violating a duty of office (Arts. 275, 280). Broad "public official" definition. Sources: uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1529 (official, identified) + Charles Russell Speechlys 2026 guide + Lexis Middle East. Confidence: corroborated secondary.
1.2 Self-reporting exemption for briber/intermediary who reports before discovery: exists; secondary sources split on the article number (281 vs 284; one stream also surfaced 239). DO NOT cite an article number before checking the official text. Confidence: existence corroborated; article number unresolved.
1.3 Embezzlement: Article 260 (embezzlement by public employee of money/documents in possession by virtue of position); Article 261 (unlawful appropriation of public funds not in possession). Asset seizure and penalties ≥ amounts embezzled. Confidence: corroborated secondary.
1.4 Abuse of office: criminalised (and Abu Dhabi Law 6/2016 Art. 53 covers abuse of position by Abu Dhabi government employees); Penal Code exact article number: No sufficient public evidence identified.
1.5 Duty to report crimes: Article 323 imposes a positive reporting obligation (per HSF Kramer 2025). Confidence: corroborated secondary.

## Area 2 — Whistleblower protection
2.1 Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 (AML/CFT): Article 15 protects institutions, board members, employees for good-faith STR reporting (with Art. 17.3 of Cabinet Decision 10/2019); Article 27 extends good-faith protection (per compliance sources) "unless made in bad faith or with the intent of causing damages." Source: centralbank.ae official PDF (identified). Corroborated secondary.
2.2 No comprehensive federal whistleblower protection statute as of 2025 sources; protection is sector- and emirate-specific. Federal general whistleblower law 2023–2026: No sufficient public evidence identified. Adjacent: Dubai Executive Council Resolution No. 2 of 2025 (employment protection of public-sector whistleblowers, FAA jurisdiction).
2.3 Dubai Law No. 4 of 2016 (DESC), amended by Law 9/2019: whistleblower = person who notifies or cooperates with DESC; protected against prosecution and workplace retaliation; disclosure to DESC not a breach of NDA/confidentiality. First UAE law of its kind. Corroborated secondary.
2.4 DIFC: Operating Law 7/2018 as amended by DIFC Law No. 2 of 2022 — good-faith disclosers "shall not be dismissed from employment or otherwise subject to victimization"; contravention fine USD 30,000. DFSA whistleblowing regime effective 7 April 2022 — first regulatory whistleblowing regime in the UAE. Corroborated secondary.
2.5 ADGM Whistleblower Protection Regulations 2024 (exact name verified): in force 5 July 2024; transition deadline 31 May 2025. Protected Disclosures = good-faith reports of reasonably suspected breaches of ADGM-administered legislation or financial crime; protection covers civil and contractual liability and any form of detriment including termination; anonymous good-faith reporting protected; non-retaliation integrated into ADGM Employment Regulations 2024; record-keeping ≥ 6 years. Corroborated secondary (official mirror + multiple firms).
2.6 UAE Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law 33/2021) Article 47: termination unlawful if because the worker filed a serious complaint with MoHRE or a valid lawsuit; compensation up to 3 months' wage. Complaint-based, not a general whistleblowing regime. Corroborated secondary (u.ae).

## Area 3 — Public-sector conduct rules
3.1 Federal Decree-Law 49/2022 (HR in Federal Government) + Cabinet Resolution No. 79 of 2023 (Code of Professional Conduct and Ethics for the Public Service): integrity, COI (no misuse of position for personal/family benefit), confidentiality, government-property stewardship; gifts barred except symbolic/promotional items bearing the giver's name/emblem, with a designated organisational unit to receive gifts (earlier code: Cabinet Resolution 15/2010, COI to relatives to 4th degree). Discipline per the HR law's violations chapter. Sources: fahr.gov.ae PDFs, uaelegislation.gov.ae/2112, u.ae. Corroborated secondary.
3.2 Abu Dhabi Law No. 6 of 2016 (Human Resources in the Emirate of Abu Dhabi): governs Abu Dhabi civil service; Article 53 prohibits abuse of position and corruption; disciplinary framework in the law and implementing regulations. Sources: scad.gov.ae library; dge.gov.ae/en/laws-resolutions-circulars; Lexology/Chambers. Corroborated secondary.
3.3 Published Abu Dhabi emirate-wide code of ethics document (beyond Law 6/2016 and entity codes): No sufficient public evidence identified this session — note ADAA publishes an "Abu Dhabi Code of Ethics" for its subject entities (see Stream 1 context); check dge.gov.ae laws page directly.

## Area 4 — Institutional architecture
4.1 CORRECTION: the federal supreme audit body is established by Federal Decree-Law No. 56 of 2023 (UAE/Emirates Accountability Authority, announced 19 December 2023) — NOT "Federal Law 23/2022" (that number amends the Central Bank law). Replaced the Supreme Audit Institution (Federal Law 8/2011); "the supreme authority for financial control, auditing, integrity and transparency"; reports to the UAE President. Corroborated secondary.
4.2 ADAA (context only, excluded as benchmark): Abu Dhabi Law No. 14 of 2008 (renaming the Abu Dhabi Audit Authority); independent, reports to the Crown Prince; audits Abu Dhabi government entities and companies ≥25% government-owned (500+ subject entities). Amending-law numbers: No sufficient public evidence identified.
4.3 Public prosecution specialized units: Dubai Public Funds Prosecution operates jointly with DESC (e.g., AED 180m crypto money-laundering network disruption). Broader mapping: No sufficient public evidence identified.
4.4 DESC mandate: corruption, fraud, bribery, embezzlement, damage to public property, forgery, counterfeiting, money laundering, terrorism financing; jurisdiction over Dubai government agencies, subsidised entities and licensed businesses including free zones; powers to request police/Public Prosecution assistance, seize property, coordinate internationally. Corroborated secondary.

## Area 5 — International standing
- UNCAC ratified by Federal Decree No. 8 of 2006 (signed August 2005, ratified February 2006). Corroborated secondary.
- FATF: 2020 MER → grey-listed 4 March 2022 → removed 23 February 2024 after completing the action plan; EU later removed UAE from its high-risk AML list (2025). Well-triangulated secondary.
- Transparency International CPI: CPI 2024 score 68 — highest among Arab states (TI MENA release). CPI 2025 reported 69 points, rank ~21 (aggregator citing TI — verify rank on transparency.org before citing).
- Arab Anti-Corruption Convention (League of Arab States, 21 December 2010): UAE signatory. GCC-specific anti-corruption treaty: No sufficient public evidence identified.

## Area 6 — Procurement integrity
6.1 Abu Dhabi Procurement Standards: issued by the Department of Government Support (Office of Government Procurement) under Abu Dhabi Law No. 4 of 2020; function now sits with DGE (dge.gov.ae/en/what-we-do/procurement). Unify procurement rules for all Abu Dhabi government entities; address conflicts of interest (no-COI undertakings, NDAs in the procurement cycle) and mandate transparency/integrity. Sources: mediaoffice.abudhabi launch; full PDF mirror (ku.ac.ae); dge.gov.ae. Corroborated secondary.
6.2 Federal Law No. 11 of 2023 on Procurement in the Federal Government (+ Cabinet Resolution executive regulation): aims at "integrity, trust, transparency, and competitiveness"; defines actual and potential COI (personal interests incl. family to 4th degree and personal/social relations); prohibits federal-entity employees from participating in their own entity's procurements directly, indirectly, or via companies they own/partner/represent. Sources: mof.gov.ae official PDF; uaelegislation.gov.ae/2709. Corroborated secondary.

## Area 7 — Corporate governance whistleblowing requirements
7.1 SCA Chairman Decision No. (3/R.M) of 2020 — Corporate Governance Guide for PJSCs (in force 28 April 2020; amended by Decision (2/R.M) of 2024, effective 16 January 2024): audit committee must review whistleblower reports on financial matters and monitor compliance with anti-fraud/anti-corruption policies; confidential reporting arrangements required. Exact article number: No sufficient public evidence identified — verify in the official guide. (Note: SCA renamed CMA effective 1 January 2026 — see Stream 2.)
7.2 CBUAE Corporate Governance Regulation for Banks (Circular No. 83/2019) + Corporate Governance Standards: boards responsible for corporate culture and values including a whistleblowing policy mechanism; issues raised form part of Board oversight. Exact article number: No sufficient public evidence identified — verify at rulebook.centralbank.ae.
