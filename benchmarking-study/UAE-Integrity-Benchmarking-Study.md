## 1. Executive Summary

**Where are we today?** The Integrity Section operates at a provisional 57 of 100 on this study's scoring model — an Established function with a sharply uneven profile. Its investigative method, fraud risk assessment programme (312 fraud risks, 497 control owners, 8 registers, 41 entities) and executive reporting run at Advanced level. Everything that requires owned infrastructure runs at Developing or below: the Tahadath channel is confidential with an anti-retaliation commitment and a published seven-category violation taxonomy, but public evidence shows no anonymity option, no case tracking for reporters, no two-way communication and no independent hosting; case management is manual; conflict-of-interest work is per-case, not programmatic; gifts and hospitality machinery is not evidenced at all. The function's deepest structural weakness is that its maturity is embodied in one practitioner's codified method rather than in section procedures, systems and staffing. (Current-state register: carried from the project record; Tahadath attributes: search-verified from dge.gov.ae.)

**Who are the leading UAE benchmarks?** Five entities, chosen for practice strength, evidence and transferability rather than size: **Mubadala** (67/100 — the only UAE entity evidenced with ISO 37001, ISO 37301 and ISO 37002 together, and a dedicated Ethics & Compliance Office that itself investigates), **DFSA** (65 — the most fully documented investigation-governance model: dedicated enforcement, a Decision Making Committee whose members had "no previous direct involvement in establishing the evidence," external tribunal appeal), **ADGM** (63 — the first statutory whistleblower-protection regime in Abu Dhabi, in force July 2024, mandatory entity arrangements since May 2025), **Dubai Financial Audit Authority** (63 — the closest public-sector model: Nazaha public reporting, statutory employment protection for reporters under Decision 2/2025, an anti-fraud framework, live oversight dashboards), and **ADNOC** (59 — the reference whistleblowing platform: Takallam, run by an independent Dutch operator, anonymous, two-way, tri-channel, multilingual, open to the public). Etihad (case tracking by Report Key; annual COI declaration cycle), Emirates NBD (whistleblowing transparency metrics in annual reports; a named Head of Fraud Prevention and Investigation) and FAB (named vendor, unique case numbers, WhatsApp channel) are dimension-specific benchmarks. All scores are calculated in session from disclosed evidence and measure disclosed maturity.

**What are they doing differently?** Four things, consistently. They put the reporting channel outside the organization or under certification, with anonymity and a way to keep talking to the reporter. They separate investigation from decision structurally, not just procedurally. They publish — enforcement outcomes, report counts, thematic reviews — and treat transparency as a control. And the government side (UAEAA, FAA, ADGM) anchors reporter protection in an instrument, not a webpage promise.

**What are our main gaps?** Channel capability (anonymity, tracking, two-way, independence); no case-management system; no documented investigation charter or manual despite an operating method that exceeds most disclosed peers; no entity-wide COI declaration cycle; no evidenced gifts register; no standing analytics; single-practitioner dependency.

**What are the biggest risks?** A reporter deterred by the channel's disclosed limits takes the allegation to Wajib or nowhere; a key-person departure removes the investigative capability entirely; manual case handling cannot evidence SLA, audit trail or trend patterns when the Accountability Authority or the Chairman asks; and prevention (COI, gifts) stays reactive while procurement exposure — the section's own caseload shows it — is the live risk family.

**What should we implement immediately (0–6 months)?** Upgrade Tahadath's disclosed capability set (anonymity option, case reference number, status states, evidence upload) or procure a hosted channel; issue the Integrity Section charter and investigation mandate; convert the operating method into an approved investigation manual and case register; start the entity-wide COI annual declaration and a gifts register under the federal code model; add whistleblowing metrics to the section's executive reporting.

**What over 2–3 years?** A case-management system with workflow, SLA and audit trail; risk-intelligence analytics over procurement and HR data feeding the FRA cycle; an ISO 37001/37301 certification path with ISO 37002-conformant channel governance; a second and third investigator with defined competency and certification requirements; AI-assisted triage — a genuine white space: no UAE entity publicly evidences NLP triage of whistleblowing reports today, while Abu Dhabi government already operates AI incident routing in TAMM.

**What should the future Integrity Function look like?** An integrity function with a documented mandate, three sub-functions (prevention, reporting-channel and case management, investigations), structural separation between investigation and decision through a violations committee, a certified channel, system-based casework, analytics feeding a risk-led annual plan, and reporting to the Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee with unrestricted access to the Chairman — the Target Operating Model in Section 21.

## 2. Objective and Scope

**Objective.** Identify the entities and practices in the UAE — Abu Dhabi first — that constitute the benchmark for a government integrity function across thirteen domains (integrity, anti-corruption, whistleblowing, fraud risk management, internal investigations, ethics, compliance, conflict of interest, gifts and hospitality, case management, governance, integrity risk management, technology and AI); compare the current function against them; and derive an implementable development path: gaps, priorities, target operating model, roadmap and executive recommendations.

**Geographic scope.** Abu Dhabi emirate entities (government, semi-government, regulatory, sovereign); UAE federal entities; other-emirate entities (mainly Dubai) where a practice merits comparison. International references only where no strong local benchmark exists (Sections 6.5, 13.4).

**Mandatory exclusions (honoured throughout).** The Abu Dhabi Accountability Authority is not used as a primary benchmark or comparison reference; it appears only as institutional context (its mandate over Abu Dhabi entities and >25% government-owned companies, its Wajib platform origin, its AI-audit investment). Abu Dhabi Police is not used as an investigations benchmark.

**What this study is not.** It is not an assessment of any entity's actual internal effectiveness. Public-domain benchmarking measures *disclosed* maturity; a low score can mean weak disclosure rather than weak practice, and every "Not evidenced" flag is a statement about the public record.

### 6.2 What makes a platform leading practice (evidence-derived)

Independent operation (a named third party or certified management system); anonymity that survives dialogue (two-way communication while anonymous — SpeakUp's model at ADNOC); a case identity the reporter keeps (Etihad's Report Key, FAB's case number); multi-channel and multilingual access; scope beyond employees (public, suppliers, customers); an anti-retaliation commitment anchored in an instrument, not a webpage; and published evidence the system is used and supervised.

### 6.3 Tier definitions applied

- **Basic** — an address: email or form, confidentiality promised, nothing else disclosed (ADCB, e&, MOEI).
- **Developed** — a dedicated named channel with taxonomy and anti-retaliation, in-house, no anonymity mechanics or tracking (Trust Voice, **Tahadath**, Emirates Group, TAQA, Aldar, DP World, Emirates NBD).
- **Advanced** — anonymity or encryption or statutory protection or incentives, plus institutional governance (CBUAE, Wajib, ADGM, DFSA regime, Nazaha, Raqeeb, Mubadala).
- **Leading Practice** — independent operation + anonymity + two-way/tracking + multi-stakeholder + multilingual (ADNOC, Etihad, FAB).

### 6.4 Position of Tahadath

Tahadath is a genuine dedicated integrity channel — confidential, anti-retaliation, a published seven-category taxonomy, legal-alignment statement — which already places DGE ahead of most Abu Dhabi government departments, where no channel is publicly evidenced at all. Its disclosed gap set is precisely the leading-practice attribute list: anonymity option, independent hosting, case number and status states, two-way communication, evidence upload, public/customer scope. Closing that set is a bounded, known-technology change.

### 6.5 International reference (only where no local benchmark exists)

Post-report treatment monitoring (systematically tracking whether a reporter suffers detriment after reporting) is weak even in the DIFC data and unevidenced elsewhere in the UAE. The EU Whistleblowing Directive model (acknowledgement within 7 days, feedback within 3 months, retaliation reversal of burden of proof) is the relevant external reference for designing that mechanic. Register: carried from general knowledge, not verified this session — verify before citing in any final deliverable.

### 7.2 The five organizational models evidenced in the UAE, with trade-offs

1. **Regulator-embedded enforcement with structural separation** (DFSA, FSRA, CBUAE). Investigators sit in a dedicated function; sanction decisions go to a body with no prior involvement (DFSA DMC), with external appeal (FMT). *For:* independence at the decision point, published accountability, defensibility. *Against:* heavy apparatus; transfers to a department as a committee design, not a full copy.
2. **Standalone specialized agency** (DESC). *For:* full independence, police-grade powers, protection attached to cooperation. *Against:* exists only at emirate level; a department refers to it, it does not replicate it.
3. **External oversight body with investigation arm** (UAEAA; Dubai FAA; ADAA as Abu Dhabi context). *For:* entities gain a referral gateway with prosecution reach and public-funds recovery. *Against:* it defines what the internal function must hand over, not how the internal function works.
4. **Committee-based internal adjudication** (federal Decree-Law 49/2022 and Dubai HR law): each entity's violations committee adjudicates (Dubai: within 30 days); the investigation preceding the committee is codified centrally but no government entity publicly describes a standing internal investigation unit. *For:* legal anchoring, due process. *Against:* investigation capability itself is invisible and therefore unstandardised — this is the layer the Integrity Section occupies and can lead in.
5. **Compliance-owned corporate model** (Mubadala explicitly; ADNOC, Etihad, FAB by ownership statements; Emirates NBD with a named fraud-investigation head). *For:* clear ownership, third-party intake, policy integration. *Against:* independence rests on management structure; methodology unpublished.

**Where should investigations sit?** No UAE public evidence supports HR- or Legal-owned investigation as a leading model; the evidenced leading pattern is a dedicated function (integrity/compliance/enforcement) that investigates, with adjudication separated into a committee and referral gateways to the oversight body and prosecution. That is the model the TOM adopts (Section 21).

### 7.3 The finding behind the table

No UAE government entity publicly documents an internal investigation unit's reporting line or methodology; no UAE government entity publicly references ACFE standards, chain of custody or interview standards; corporate disclosure stops at intake and ownership. Consequence: a department that *documents* its investigation mandate, manual, evidence standards and QA would hold a publicly defensible position no evidenced local peer occupies. The section's operating method (record-based: reconciliation batteries, exhibit custody with md5, withdrawal discipline, calibrated language, referral formulas) is already at or above the disclosed field; what is missing is its institutionalisation.

## 9. Conflict of Interest Benchmark

Evidenced practices, strongest first:

1. **Etihad** — a named COI Programme covering staff and Board, with an Annual Conflict of Interest Declaration Cycle (official careers-page description of the compliance function). The only UAE entity evidenced running an annual declaration *cycle*.
2. **Federal code (FAHR / Cabinet Resolution 79/2023)** — avoidance of actual and potential COI, family scope to the fourth degree, right to request transfer, unresolved cases routed to HR; a ready normative base for any entity programme.
3. **Federal Procurement Law 11/2023** — defines actual and potential COI including personal/social relations, and prohibits employees from participating in their own entity's procurement directly or indirectly. Abu Dhabi Procurement Standards (DGE-administered) carry no-COI undertakings and NDAs in the procurement cycle.
4. **Aldar** — board-level: immediate written disclosure, abstention from decision-making, Audit Committee pre-approval bright lines.
5. **Mubadala/ADNOC** — code-level declaration duties with E&C escalation.

**Not evidenced anywhere in the UAE public domain:** a digital COI platform with automated reminders, risk-based review, mitigation-plan workflow, or integration with HR/procurement/finance systems. This is a second white space: the components exist as law and code text, not as evidenced systems. (International reference, carried from general knowledge, unverified this session: OECD manages COI as a lifecycle — declare, assess, mitigate, monitor — which is the design logic the TOM adopts.)

**Current state (record-based):** COI checks are casework findings (committee declarations absent; role combinations designed in) and preventive recommendations. No entity-wide declaration cycle in the record. Gap: programmatic COI is missing, and the section's own casework proves the cost of its absence.

## 10. Gifts and Hospitality Benchmark

Public evidence is the thinnest of all dimensions — itself a finding:

1. **Federal code model (FAHR)** — the strongest evidenced rule set: no gifts, hospitality or services for public servants or family to the fourth degree where any obligation or influence on objectivity results; acceptance limited to symbolic/promotional items bearing the giver's name; **a designated organisational unit receives gifts**. This is a complete design for a departmental gifts register.
2. **Aldar** — bright lines: "Gifts in the form of cash are never allowed"; Audit Committee pre-approval for directors.
3. **ADNOC** — qualitative tests (legitimate business reason, proportionality, influence intent).
4. **Numeric AED thresholds:** no UAE entity publishes one. **Digital gifts and hospitality management systems:** No sufficient public evidence identified at any UAE entity.

**Current state (record-based):** no gifts and hospitality work appears in the record (NE — a record statement, not proof the Department lacks rules; the Abu Dhabi HR framework under Law 6/2016 applies). Quick win: a section-administered gifts register on the federal-code design, with pre-approval workflow and procurement-linked red flags.

## 12. Case Management Benchmark

Lifecycle assessed: Report → Intake → Triage → Preliminary Assessment → Risk Rating → Assignment → Investigation → Evidence → Findings → Decision → Corrective Action → Closure → Lessons Learned.

Evidenced practice is fragmentary — no UAE entity publicly documents an end-to-end case-management system for integrity cases:

- **Intake and classification:** FAB's published taxonomy and case numbers; Tahadath's seven categories; DFSA's assess-and-escalate duty.
- **Assignment and secure filing:** Emirates NBD ("assigns cases to a competent member... reports are filed and evidence is maintained under secure and confidential conditions").
- **Decision separation and deadlines:** Dubai ECR 4/2019 — violations committee must decide within 30 days, one extension, judicial referral carve-out. The only evidenced SLA anywhere in the register.
- **Dashboards:** FAA Al Mo'ashir consolidating "instances of financial and administrative misconduct" into oversight analytics.
- **Lessons learned:** the section's own quality-log and withdrawal discipline (record-based) is the only evidenced instance of the loop operating.

**Current state (record-based):** manual and document-driven with reference-level traceability (status vocabulary, linkage registers, cross-counts, md5 custody) and no system: no workflow, automated assignment, SLA tracking, dashboard or cross-case trend analysis. The paradox to preserve: the section's manual discipline exceeds what most systems enforce; the system must encode that discipline, not replace it.

## 21. Target Operating Model (future-state)

**Mandate.** A chartered Integrity Office holding four mandates: prevention (COI, gifts, awareness, integrity risk), reporting channel and case management, investigations, and integrity analytics — anchored in an approved charter naming powers (records access, interview authority, referral duties) and boundaries.

**Structure.** Three units under the Head of Integrity: (1) Prevention & Risk (FRA cycle, COI programme, gifts register, awareness, culture measurement); (2) Intake & Case Management (channel operation, triage, case system, SLA, analytics); (3) Investigations (≥2 trained investigators, manual-governed, QA on every file). Analytics capability shared across units.

**Reporting line and independence.** Functionally to the Audit, Risk and Compliance Committee with unrestricted access to the Chairman; administratively within the Internal Audit and Risk Management Sector. Case decisions by a Violations Committee whose members have no investigative involvement (DFSA-principle, federal-committee form); the Integrity Office investigates and presents, never adjudicates alone; closure authority sits with the committee; classification overrides recorded with reasons in the case system.

**Interfaces (no jurisdictional overlap):**
- **HR** — receives employment-grievance referrals (jurisdiction test at triage, as current record practice); executes disciplinary decisions; supplies post-report treatment data; never investigates integrity allegations.
- **Legal** — advises on characterisation and referral instruments; owns litigation; no case decision role.
- **Internal Audit** — control weaknesses flow both ways; audit does not investigate misconduct; integrity findings feed the audit plan.
- **Risk** — FRA integrates with ERM registers (already the record practice); KRIs co-owned.
- **Compliance/Governance** — policy suite ownership coordinated; code administration joint.
- **Procurement** — COI gate, gifts red flags, supplier-code alignment (AD Procurement Standards); integrity sign-off on high-risk awards.
- **Finance** — payment-pattern data feed; recovery actions execution.
- **Executive Management** — receives the KPI pack; decides on committee recommendations within defined authorities.
- **External** — referral gateways: Accountability Authority (standing formula, already approved wording in the record), prosecution via the Department's legal channel.

**Technology.** Case system as the spine; channel platform integrated; dashboards for ARCC; data feeds (procurement, HR, finance) for KRIs; NLP-assisted intake classification as Phase 3, human decision mandatory; every AI use logged and reviewable under UAE AI-governance principles.

**Reporting.** Quarterly ARCC pack: channel KPIs, case pipeline with SLA states, FRA movement, COI/gifts statistics, culture pulse annually; the existing money-table-first, one-finding-per-slide discipline retained as the house standard.

## 25. Executive Conclusion

The UAE benchmark field divides cleanly: regulators and oversight bodies lead on investigation governance and statutory reporter protection; state-linked corporates lead on channel capability and certified management systems; no entity leads everywhere, and several dimensions — digital COI platforms, gifts systems, culture measurement, AI triage — are white space across the whole field. The Integrity Section enters this comparison with an unusual profile: its investigative substance and risk-assessment scale already stand at the Advanced level of the field, while its infrastructure sits below the Developed line. The development path is therefore not a maturity climb from zero; it is the conversion of an embodied practice into an institution — charter, committee, channel, system, programme — in that order. Executed on the 2026–2028 roadmap, the function moves from 57 to a defensible mid-70s profile on this model, with three artefacts no evidenced local government peer holds: a published investigation methodology, a measured integrity culture, and AI-assisted intake under human decision.
